## 1. Trend Analysis — `workspace/output/trend_result.csv`

Using Sen's slope with the Mann-Kendall test (16 years, 2006–2022):

| slope (°C/year) | p-value |
|---|---|

**Yes, there is a statistically significant long-term warming trend** (p < 0.05): the lake surface warms by ~0.09 °C per year. (For reference, linear regression gave slope = 0.0805, p = 0.0548 — marginally significant; Sen's slope is the more robust estimate for environmental data.)

## 2. Dominant Driving Factor — `workspace/output/dominant_factor.csv`

| variable | contribution |
|---|---|
| Heat | 53.21 |

**Heat is the most important category, contributing ~53% of the explained variance in water temperature.**

Method: the 9 variables were classified as Heat (AirTempLake, Shortwave, Longwave), Flow (Precip, Inflow, Outflow), Wind (WindSpeedLake), Human (DevelopedArea, AgricultureArea), then a single global PCA with varimax rotation (4 factors) was run on all variables, and R² decomposition (drop-one-factor) was applied to the factor scores:

- **Heat: 53.2%** (dominated by the air-temperature/longwave component)
- Human: 3.4%
- Flow: 0.2%
- Wind: ~0%

The full model explains ~58% of water-temperature variance, and the heat component — primarily AirTempLake — accounts for the large majority of it, consistent with the physical expectation that air temperature is the main energy input to lake surface water.
